# Keywords/Relevance vs. Authority: How They Rank a Page Together

Google ranking isn't a single formula where relevance and authority get weighted and summed. It works more like a **two-stage funnel**: relevance decides who gets *considered*, authority decides who *wins* among those considered.

```
All pages on the web
        ↓
STAGE 1 — Relevance Filter
Pages that plausibly answer the query
(semantic match + intent match + adequate depth)
        ↓
STAGE 2 — Authority Ranking
Rank the filtered pool by trust/authority signals
        ↓
Final search results order
```

---

## Part 1: Relevance / Keywords — The Gatekeeper

Relevance determines whether a page is even eligible to rank. Google evaluates this through:

- **Semantic/topical matching** — via NLP models (BERT, MUM) that understand meaning, not just literal keyword matches. A page can rank without the exact query phrase if it thoroughly covers the topic.
- **Search intent matching** — informational, navigational, transactional, or commercial-investigation. The *format* of your content must match what the searcher wants, not just the topic.
- **Content depth/comprehensiveness** — pages that cover a topic's full breadth (related subtopics, common follow-ups) outperform pages narrowly targeting one keyword.
- **On-page structure** — title tags, H1s, and early content act as *confirmation* signals, not primary drivers.

**Key trait:** Relevance is close to binary at the top. Once a page clears the "is this actually about the query" bar, further keyword optimization has diminishing returns — you can't out-keyword your way to #1 in a competitive niche.

---

## Part 2: Authority — The Tiebreaker

Authority decides ranking order *among* pages that already passed the relevance filter:

- **Link-based authority** — links act as endorsements; endorsements from well-trusted, topically-relevant sites carry more weight (PageRank-style, recursive).
- **Domain-level trust** — accumulated over time (age, consistency, clean history). A new page on an established domain often outranks a similar page on a brand-new domain.
- **E-E-A-T** — Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness. Author credentials, first-hand experience, citations, transparency. Weighs more heavily for YMYL topics (health, finance, legal).
- **Brand/entity recognition** — Google's Knowledge Graph increasingly trusts recognized brands/entities independent of raw backlink count.

**Key trait:** Authority does most of the sorting *within* the relevant pool — but it never gets a chance to matter if the page didn't clear the relevance bar first.

---

## Where They Complement Each Other

| Scenario | What happens |
|---|---|
| High relevance, low authority | Can rank well for low-competition, long-tail queries where the "relevant pool" is small |
| Low relevance, high authority | Backlinks don't help — page may not even enter the Stage 1 pool |
| High relevance, high authority | Best combination — clears the filter easily *and* wins the tiebreak |
| Competitive query, thin relevance pool | Google raises the relevance bar itself, so authority alone can't compensate for weak content |

**The complementary relationship:**
1. **Relevance earns entry; authority earns position.** Strong content gets you into the race. Backlinks and trust decide where you place.
2. **Authority signals often *depend* on relevance.** A backlink from a topically-relevant, authoritative site (e.g., a fitness blog linking to a protein-powder page) counts far more than a link from an unrelated site — so authority quality is itself partly a relevance function.
3. **Good content earns links naturally.** Comprehensive, genuinely useful content is what other sites choose to cite — meaning strong relevance work compounds into authority over time.
4. **Competition level shifts the balance.** Low competition → relevance alone can win. High competition → Google raises the entry bar *and* authority does more of the sorting — you need both.

---

## Practical Strategy: Using Both to Rank Higher

1. **Start with relevance** — you can't skip this step. Build genuinely comprehensive content that matches real search intent (not just keyword-matched, but format-matched).
2. **Target long-tail queries first** — smaller relevant pools mean content quality alone can win rankings while your site is still building authority.
3. **Earn topically-relevant backlinks** — prioritize a handful of links from respected sites in your niche over large volumes of generic links.
4. **Let authority compound** — as strong content earns natural citations and backlinks, your domain-level trust rises, making it easier to rank *even your future, newer pages* faster.
5. **For competitive/YMYL topics, invest in both simultaneously** — content depth and E-E-A-T signals (author bios, citations, transparency) plus deliberate outreach for authoritative backlinks.

**Bottom line:** Relevance and authority aren't competing levers to balance — they're sequential filters that reinforce each other. Relevant content is what makes authority achievable; authority is what makes relevant content rank at the top instead of page two.
